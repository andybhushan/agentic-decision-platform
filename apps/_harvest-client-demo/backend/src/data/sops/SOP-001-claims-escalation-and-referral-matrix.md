# SOP-001 — Claims Escalation & Referral Matrix

## 1. Purpose
This procedure standardises how a claim is referred or escalated for specialist
review or additional authority. It exists to ensure referrals are timely, that
adjusters are never blocked from raising a fraud or coverage concern, and that
supervisory sign-off is applied only where it genuinely adds control value.

It aligns with Chubb's claims handling principles: fair, prompt, and transparent
claim resolution, with clear ownership and auditable decision-making.

## 2. Core Principle — Two Independent Escalation Axes
There are two **separate and independent** reasons to involve someone beyond the
handling adjuster. They must not be conflated:

- **Specialist referral (skills-based):** the claim needs an expert lens — SIU
  for suspected fraud, Legal for coverage disputes, Medical for injury review.
  These referrals are **direct**. Any adjuster may raise them immediately,
  regardless of seniority, and **do not** require Claims Supervisor sign-off.
- **Authority escalation (limit-based):** the settlement or exposure amount
  exceeds the handling adjuster's delegated authority limit (see SOP-002). Only
  then is a Claims Supervisor (or higher) required — to approve the financial
  decision, not to permit the specialist referral.

A single claim can trigger both axes at once (e.g. a suspected-fraud claim whose
exposure also exceeds the adjuster's limit). In that case the SIU referral
proceeds directly **and** the authority escalation happens in parallel.

## 3. Referral Matrix
| Trigger | Refer to | Direct? | Supervisor sign-off required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suspected fraud / staged-loss indicators | SIU (Special Investigations Unit) | Yes | No |
| Coverage dispute / policy-language ambiguity | Legal / Coverage Counsel | Yes | No |
| Significant or disputed bodily injury | Medical Review | Yes | No |
| Settlement/exposure over adjuster's authority limit | Claims Supervisor then Manager | N/A | Yes (financial approval) |
| Litigation served or threatened | Legal | Yes | No |
| Regulatory / complaint / conduct concern | Claims Governance Office | Yes | No |

## 4. Routing Rules for the Digital Steward
When advising an adjuster, the Digital Steward MUST:

1. Recommend specialist referrals (SIU / Legal / Medical) **directly** and never
   state that Claims Supervisor approval is required to make one.
2. Recommend Claims Supervisor involvement **only** when the claim's
   settlement/exposure exceeds the active adjuster's delegated authority limit
   (from the adjuster persona in context), or where SOP-002 otherwise requires it.
3. When both apply, name the specialist referral as the primary action and note
   the authority-limit escalation as a separate, parallel requirement.
4. Never imply that a more junior adjuster must "route up" a fraud or coverage
   concern through a supervisor before it can reach a specialist.

## 5. Timeliness
- Suspected fraud: refer to SIU within **1 business day** of the indicator being
  identified.
- Coverage dispute: refer to Legal before issuing any coverage position to the
  claimant.
- Authority escalation: raise before communicating or committing any settlement
  that exceeds the adjuster's limit.

## 6. Audit & Records
Every referral and escalation is logged on the claim file with the trigger,
target, timestamp, and rationale. The Digital Steward's recommendations are
advisory and are recorded alongside the adjuster's action for compliance review.
