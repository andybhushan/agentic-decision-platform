# SOP-002 — Delegated Settlement Authority

## 1. Purpose
Delegated authority ensures each claim decision is made at the appropriate level.
It protects policyholders and the business by matching financial exposure to the
right level of experience and oversight, while keeping routine claims moving
without unnecessary escalation.

## 2. Authority Tiers
The following delegated settlement authority limits apply. Limits are the maximum
total settlement / committed exposure an adjuster may approve **without** further
sign-off.

| Seniority | Delegated settlement authority |
|---|---|
| Junior | up to $5,000 |
| Senior | up to $15,000–$25,000 (assigned individually) |
| Principal | up to $50,000 |
| Lead | up to $100,000 |
| Above $100,000 | Claims Manager / Large Loss Committee |

Notes:
- Senior authority is set per individual within the $15,000–$25,000 band based on
  line of business and track record; use the specific limit on the adjuster's
  profile, not the band, when one is provided.
- The relevant figure is the **total** settlement or committed exposure on the
  claim, inclusive of indemnity and known expense, not the amount of any single
  payment.

## 3. The Authority Rule
1. If the total settlement / exposure is **at or below** the adjuster's limit, the
   adjuster may proceed within normal claims handling (subject to any specialist
   referral required by SOP-001).
2. If it **exceeds** the adjuster's limit, the decision must be approved by the
   next authority tier (Claims Supervisor, then Manager, then Large Loss
   Committee) **before** it is communicated to any party or committed on the file.
3. Authority escalation approves the **financial decision only**. It is not a
   prerequisite for, and does not replace, any SIU / Legal / Medical referral.

## 4. Guidance for the Digital Steward
- Compare the claim's estimated settlement / exposure against the **active
  adjuster's** authority limit provided in context.
- If exposure is within limit: do not recommend Claims Supervisor involvement on
  authority grounds.
- If exposure is over limit: recommend authority escalation to the next tier and
  state the limit and the exposure figure in the rationale.
- If the exposure is unknown, say so and recommend confirming the reserve/exposure
  before any settlement step, rather than assuming a supervisor is required.

## 5. Worked Examples
- **Principal, $50,000 limit, $18,000 settlement, suspected fraud:** refer to SIU
  directly (SOP-003); no authority escalation — exposure is within limit.
- **Junior, $5,000 limit, $12,000 settlement:** adjuster may progress handling but
  must escalate the settlement decision for approval above their $5,000 limit.
- **Lead, $100,000 limit, $140,000 exposure, injury dispute:** refer to Medical
  Review directly (SOP-005) **and** escalate the settlement to Claims
  Manager / Committee as it exceeds the $100,000 limit.

## 6. Audit & Records
Authority escalations, the approving tier, and the approved amount are recorded on
the claim file and are auditable by compliance.
