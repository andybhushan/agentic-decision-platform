# SOP-005 — Injury & Medical Review

## 1. Purpose
Bodily-injury claims require medical expertise to assess causation, severity,
prognosis, and reasonable treatment. This SOP sets when a claim must be referred
for Medical Review so that injury settlements are fair, evidence-based, and
defensible.

## 2. Direct Referral Principle
Medical Review referral is **direct** and available to any adjuster regardless of
seniority. It does not require Claims Supervisor sign-off. Authority escalation
(SOP-002) is separate and only applies if the injury settlement/exposure exceeds
the adjuster's limit.

## 3. When to Refer for Medical Review
- Significant, permanent, or life-changing injury is claimed.
- Injury severity, causation, or prognosis is disputed or unclear.
- Treatment appears disproportionate to the reported mechanism of injury.
- Pre-existing conditions may affect causation or apportionment.
- The injury quantum is a material driver of the claim's exposure.

Minor, well-documented, and undisputed injuries handled within normal guidance do
not require Medical Review.

## 4. Handling Rules
1. Do not finalise injury quantum on a significant or disputed injury before
   Medical Review is complete.
2. Gather the relevant medical evidence (reports, records, treatment plans) and
   summarise the causation and severity questions for the reviewer.
3. Keep any injury-related reserve aligned with the current medical picture and
   update it as evidence develops.
4. Treat all medical information as sensitive personal data and share it only with
   those who need it for the review.

## 5. Guidance for the Digital Steward
- When significant or disputed injury is present, recommend a **direct Medical
  Review referral** and identify the specific causation/severity question.
- Never recommend settling injury quantum before Medical Review on a significant
  or disputed injury.
- Only mention Claims Supervisor where the injury settlement/exposure exceeds the
  adjuster's authority limit (SOP-002), as a separate, parallel action.
- Handle claimant medical details as sensitive; do not expose them beyond what the
  advice requires.

## 6. Audit & Records
The referral, the medical question, and the review outcome are recorded on the
claim file and are auditable by compliance.
